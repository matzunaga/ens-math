Episode Three · August 23, 2026

Thirty-One

Where it speeds up · status: computed

The original conversation this continues took place March 2, 2026. This entry was written and computed today, using real code, not a transcript from that night.

This is the story of a number that grew faster than anyone expected.

Seven climbed. Thirty-one sprinted.

Same rule. Same starting point. A very different pace.

Episode two confirmed the heuristic works: swap the leading number, and the expected multiplier is that number divided by four. Seven gave 7/4. Thirty-one, the next Mersenne prime up, gives something much bigger.

leading number 31  →  expected multiplier 31/4 = 7.75  (growth, and a lot more of it)

Seven predicted mild growth. Thirty-one predicts growth more than four times as steep.

The clean case still holds, the same way it holds for every Mersenne prime, because it's a fact about the number one, not about which Mersenne prime is doing the multiplying:

1  →  32  →  16  →  8  →  4  →  2  →  1

Thirty-one plus one is exactly 32, a power of two, same as every Mersenne prime before it. Five clean halvings straight back to one. That part of the very first claim in episode one was never wrong.

Starting from three, the same starting point that carried episodes one and two, here's what actually happens with 31 in place of 7:

3  →  94  →  47  →  1458  →  729  →  22600  →  11300  →  5650  →  2825  →  87576  →  …

It doesn't just climb.

It sprints.

By step 3, it's already past 1,000.

By step 5, past 10,000.

By step 13, past 100,000.

By step 15, past a million.

By step 23, past a billion.

By step 30, past a trillion.

step 35: 1,095,275,774,340,872
sixteen digits. The same size episode two reached at step 134, reached here in a quarter of the steps.

Same starting number. Same shape of rule. A leading number more than four times bigger, and the climb got roughly four times faster to reach the same size. That's not a coincidence. That's exactly what dividing a bigger number by four predicts.

The parity count backs it up too: 14 steps where the rule multiplied by 31 and added one, 21 steps where it just halved. Fewer steps overall than episode two needed for the same size climb, because each multiply here does so much more work.

Same rule.

Same start.

A bigger number in, and the climb came back faster, steeper, and exactly on schedule.

There's a pattern hiding in plain sight now. Three gives an expected multiplier of 3/4, below one, the only case that predicts shrinking instead of growth. Every Mersenne prime after 3 starts at 7 or higher, which means every one of them divided by four gives something above one. Three isn't just special. Among Mersenne primes, three might be the only one that was ever going to behave this way.

What's known so far

Three is the only Mersenne prime smaller than four. If the heuristic holds the way it has for 7 and 31, three may be the only Mersenne prime whose swap-in ever predicts contraction instead of growth. That's an algebraic observation, not a proof about what any specific sequence actually does.
