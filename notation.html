The Collatz conjecture
first appears: episode one

A rule applied to any positive whole number, over and over: if it's even, cut it in half; if it's odd, multiply it by three and add one. The conjecture claims every number eventually reaches one, no matter where it starts. Proposed in 1937. Still unproven.

Prime number
first appears: episode one

A whole number greater than one with no even divisors, nothing splits it evenly except one and itself. 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and 13 are the first six.

Power of two
first appears: episode one

A number you reach by doubling, starting from one: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and on. Each one is exactly twice the last.

Mersenne prime
first appears: episode one

A prime number that sits exactly one below a power of two. 3 is the smallest: two times two, minus one. 7, 31, and 127 are the next few.

Expected multiplier (the heuristic argument)
first appears: episode one

A way of estimating whether a variant of the Collatz rule tends to grow or shrink, on average, without tracing any specific number by hand. On average, an odd step gets halved twice before landing on the next odd number, so the expected multiplier is the rule's leading number divided by four. Below one means shrinkage is expected. Above one means growth is expected.
