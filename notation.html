What this is. A running glossary for terms used across the ENS,math archive. Each entry is explained in plain language, links to a free outside source for anyone who wants the formal version, and includes a worked number so the definition isn't just words. New terms get added here as new episodes introduce them.
Mersenne prime
first used: episode one

A prime number that is one less than a power of two. Not every power of two minus one is prime, but when one is, it gets this name.

Read more on Wikipedia →

22 − 1 = 3  ·  23 − 1 = 7  ·  25 − 1 = 31  —  all three prime, all three Mersenne primes
Representativeness heuristic
first used: episode five

A pattern in how people judge probability: something that looks like a typical example of a category gets assumed to behave like the whole category, even when a single example is too small a sample to prove anything about the average. Borrowed here from psychology to describe the trap of expecting one computed trajectory to perfectly match a predicted average.

Read more on Wikipedia →

Twenty-seven's real average was 1.71 halvings per step. The heuristic predicted 2.00. One number isn't obligated to look like the average of every number.
Insensitivity to sample size
first used: episode five

The tendency to expect small samples to match the same odds as large ones. A coin flipped ten times doesn't owe anyone exactly five heads, and a trajectory with forty-one halving-runs doesn't owe anyone exactly the predicted average either. Small counts are allowed more spread than the long-run average promises.

Read more on Wikipedia →

41 halving-runs averaging 1.71 instead of the heuristic's predicted 2.00 isn't a broken rule, it's a small sample.
